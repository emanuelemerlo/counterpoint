# Analysis rules

What each finding of **Analyze voice** means and how to fix it. ✖ = error
(red), ▲ = warning (orange). With **Free** rules some errors become warnings or
disappear, as noted.

In the descriptions, "the counterpoint" is the voice being checked and "the
other voice" is the melody or the bass it is compared with.

## Between the voices

**✖ parallel fifths / parallel octaves** – The counterpoint and the other
voice move in the same direction from a fifth to a fifth (or octave to octave,
unison to unison). The two voices lose their independence. *Fix*: move one of
the two notes so that the voices go in contrary motion, or arrive on an
imperfect consonance (third, sixth).

**▲ hidden fifth or octave** – Arriving on a fifth or octave by similar
motion, with a leap in the upper voice, between the outer voices. *Fix*:
approach by step in the upper voice, or by contrary motion. (Not checked with
Free rules.)

**✖ voice crossing** – The counterpoint goes below the melody when it should
be above it (or above the bass, below the melody...). *Fix*: keep the voices in
their order; if there is no room, move the counterpoint by an octave.

**▲ cross-relation** – The same note in two forms, natural and altered, in two
voices close together (B♭ in one voice against B in the other). *Fix*: use the
same form in both voices, or move the chromatic note into the same voice.

**▲ doubled leading note** – The leading note (the 7th degree that pulls to the
tonic, B in C major) is in both voices over a dominant chord. Since it must
rise to the tonic, doubling it leads to parallel octaves or a poor
resolution. *Fix*: give the counterpoint another chord note.

## Dissonances

**✖ unprepared dissonance on a strong beat** – A dissonance on the beat that
is neither a prepared suspension nor a chord seventh. With Free rules it is
accepted if it resolves by step (it is an appoggiatura), and is only a warning
otherwise. *Fix*:
put a consonance on the beat, or prepare the dissonance with a tied note.

**✖ dissonance not approached or left by step** – On a weak beat a dissonance
must be a passing or neighbour note: reached and left by step. *Fix*: fill the
leap with a passing note, or use a consonance. The cambiata (a dissonance
followed by a leap of a third and a step back) and the double neighbour are
recognised and are not errors.

**✖ suspension that does not resolve down by step** – A note held while the
other voice moves has become a dissonance and does not go down one step.
*Fix*: resolve it down by step (the 7th to the 6th, the 4th to the 3rd above;
the 2nd to the 3rd below the other voice).

**▲ chord seventh that does not resolve down** – The seventh of a dominant
chord should go down by step. *Fix*: move it down one step.

## The melodic line

**✖ forbidden leap** – Augmented second, tritone, seventh, or a leap beyond the
octave. *Fix*: replace it with a smaller interval, or split it into two leaps in
the same direction that outline a consonance.

**▲ leap of a sixth** – In strict style only the ascending minor sixth is
allowed. (Not checked with Free rules.)

**▲ large leap not recovered by contrary motion** – After a leap larger than a
third the line should turn back, preferably by step. Two large leaps in the
same direction are worse. *Fix*: after the leap, move by step in the opposite
direction.

**▲ outlined tritone / outlined seventh** – A run in one direction that spans a
tritone (F–G–A–B) or a seventh: every step is fine, but the ear hears the
interval between the start and the turning point. *Fix*: change direction one
note earlier or later.

**▲ leading note that does not rise to the tonic** – Over a dominant–tonic
cadence the leading note should go up to the tonic. *Fix*: move it up one
step. (Not checked for inner voices, which may let it fall.)

**▲ climax reached again** – The highest note of the line appears more than
once. A single climax gives the line a shape. *Fix*: change the earlier or
later occurrence.

**▲ final note outside the tonic chord** – The counterpoint does not end on a
note of the tonic chord. *Fix*: end on the tonic (or the third or fifth of the
tonic chord, if the counterpoint is above).

## In the generator

The generator follows the same rules while writing and every variant is
checked with the analysis: errors and warnings appear in the list of variants
(see [Comparing variants](Comparing-variants)). With **Regenerate the rest**
the notes you kept may break a rule: the check tells you.
